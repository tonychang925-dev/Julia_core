# GIC-001 — Theme Lifecycle Cognition IR

## IDENTITY / SCOPE

**Cognition unit:** `GIC-001.theme_lifecycle`
**Primary question:** How should Julia reason when a market theme appears, gains acceptance, diffuses, diverges, repairs, accelerates, fades, or rotates?
**Transfer principle:** `strategy_transfer_without_conclusion_copy`

This unit is a reusable causal-reasoning scaffold, not a trading rule, replay verdict, or fixed state machine. Its output is a set of competing lifecycle interpretations, required observations, regime gates, counterevidence, and falsifiers. Julia must preserve the distinction between:

1. **Market facts** — persisted events, theme breadth, leader/follower behavior, relative strength, turnover, market regime, and provenance.
2. **Cognition interpretation** — which lifecycle hypothesis best explains those facts today.
3. **Trading action** — a separate decision that additionally requires market regime, leader state, setup, risk, and authorization.

### Boundaries

- A theme label is not a mainline.
- Mainline confirmation is not lifecycle state.
- Lifecycle state is not trade permission.
- A heat score is not lifecycle truth.
- One day of strength is a fact, not a durable theme conclusion.
- Historical examples transfer their reasoning structure, not their conclusion or claimed performance.

## FORMATION_PATH

Theme lifecycle cognition begins before a theme has a confirmed name and continues after public attention has peaked. The proposed formation path is:

```text
causal stimulus
→ observable theme/event cluster
→ repeated market acceptance
→ breadth and leadership formation
→ diffusion into branches / successors
→ divergence or acceleration
→ repair, rotation, or fade
→ post-event retention or death
```

### 1. Stimulus and theme candidate

A policy, conflict, technology shift, order cycle, product launch, supply shock, or social trend creates a candidate explanation. The project distinguishes a **subject/theme** from a **mainline**: themes are numerous and can last one to three days; a mainline is a rarer object that can organize risk appetite, produce leaders and branches, and evolve over weeks or months.

At this stage Julia should ask:

- Is there a causal driver or only a renamed old theme?
- Is the event specific enough to affect revenues, policy constraints, supply, demand, or capital allocation?
- Is the event new, continuing, contradicting, or only re-reported?
- Can the causal claim be falsified by subsequent company, industry, macro, or price evidence?

### 2. Fast and slow confirmation paths

The same eventual lifecycle can emerge through different confirmation paths:

- **Fast path:** a major event plus same-day market acceptance may create a machine candidate immediately, pending review.
- **Slow path:** repeated events, persistent heat, relative strength, multi-day acceptance, and leader resilience accumulate into a candidate.

The formation path affects confidence and evidence continuity, not the vocabulary used to explain later evolution. Once confirmed, both paths still require the same lifecycle questions.

### 3. Market acceptance

A theme becomes more than a news label when market participants repeatedly express it with capital. Useful observations include:

- persistence of heat rather than one-day rank;
- relative strength versus index and competing themes;
- breadth beyond one isolated stock;
- appearance of a leader or credible front row;
- capital confirmation;
- resilience after divergence or pullback;
- continuation across related subjects.

One-day strength is not sufficient. Repeated acceptance is stronger evidence, except in the explicitly provisional fast-path case.

### 4. Diffusion and hierarchy

If acceptance continues, the theme may form a hierarchy:

```text
causal core → leader/core stock → second-row stock → branch themes → laggard/complement
```

Diffusion can support the lifecycle, but its meaning is conditional. Broad and deep participation is stronger than broad-but-shallow participation. A single leader without followers is fragile. Many weak followers without a durable core may indicate attention recycling rather than a sustainable theme.

### 5. Divergence, repair, rotation, and fade

After public recognition, internal disagreement is expected. Divergence is not automatically theme death. The key question is whether capital:

- repairs the core after weakness;
- rotates from an old leader to a successor;
- moves from the core theme to a branch;
- leaves the entire family;
- never returns after support and narrative fail.

Therefore fade requires separately established evidence, not merely weakness in one stock or one session.

## MECHANISM

### Causal mechanism

The proposed mechanism is reflexive and conditional:

```text
credible causal story
→ attention and预期 formation
→ initial price/turnout confirmation
→ leader and follower evidence
→ broader capital allocation
→ branch discovery
→ feedback into more events/coverage
→ exhaustion, crowding, contradiction, or policy/supply change
→ repair, rotation, fade, or death
```

The mechanism is not "news causes a fixed duration rally." It is a feedback loop between narrative, market payment, hierarchy formation, and subsequent evidence.

### Observable evidence families

| Evidence family | Observable examples | What it can support | What it cannot prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| Event continuity | New, continuing, contradictory, or exhausted events | narrative durability | market acceptance |
| Acceptance | Multi-day strength, turnover, relative strength | capital interest | future continuation |
| Breadth | Related subjects/stocks participating | diffusion | depth or sustainability |
| Depth | Leader, front row, branch hierarchy | organization and persistence | exact future stage |
| Resilience | Recovery after divergence, pullback repair | still-alive hypothesis | permanent health |
| Failure | Support break, narrative contradiction, broad exit | fade hypothesis | whether failure was data artifact |
| Regime | Market breadth, risk appetite, volatility, style | conditional applicability | theme-specific truth |

### Interpretation mechanism

Julia should not map a score directly to a lifecycle state. A valid interpretation requires:

1. **Temporal continuity:** compare today with the preceding sequence, not only the latest rank.
2. **Conditional regime:** ask whether broad market and sentiment conditions make diffusion or failure more likely.
3. **Hierarchical structure:** identify leader, second row, branches, and laggards separately.
4. **Competing hypotheses:** keep more than one interpretation active when evidence is partial.
5. **Counterevidence:** explicitly seek facts that defeat the favored interpretation.
6. **Falsifiers:** state observations that would change the interpretation.
7. **Provenance:** distinguish missing evidence from negative evidence.

## COMPETING_HYPOTHESES

### H-A — Nascent theme / early trial

**Pattern:**

- new causal stimulus;
- limited but real market response;
- little hierarchy yet;
- event continuity untested;
- regime may be supportive or unresolved.

**Supports:** candidate status and observation requirement.
**Counterevidence:** no market response after visible stimulus; event is only recycled news; response confined to unrelated labels.
**Forbidden shortcut:** "new and strong" does not imply durable mainline.

### H-B — Fermenting / repeatedly accepted theme

**Pattern:**

- repeated events or continued causal support;
- multi-day acceptance;
- relative strength;
- emerging front row or leader;
- related subjects begin responding.

**Supports:** mainline candidacy or early lifecycle development.
**Counterevidence:** strength disappears when the market normalizes; participation is broad but shallow; leader is not credible; event chain breaks.
**Forbidden shortcut:** heat persistence alone does not prove mainline.

### H-C — Acceleration / climax risk

**Pattern:**

- unusually broad participation;
- leader extension;
- branch crowds;
- high attention and turnover;
- rapid laggard catch-up.

**Supports:** later-stage interpretation and risk-awareness.
**Counterevidence:** acceleration begins from a fresh structural driver with little prior recognition; breadth is deep rather than shallow; continuation remains organized.
**Forbidden shortcut:** high heat does not itself require immediate exit, nor does it justify automatic chasing.

### H-D — Healthy divergence or repair

**Pattern:**

- core weakens or opens lower;
- theme remains supported;
- capital repairs leader/core or successor;
- branch survives;
- market regime is not hostile.

**Supports:** still-alive divergence/repair interpretation.
**Counterevidence:** repair fails within the declared window; successor vacuum appears; support breaks broadly; event chain contradicts the story.
**Relation:** this overlaps GIC-002 Leader Divergence and GIC-003 Weak-to-Strong, but this unit asks about the whole theme; those units ask leader-relative and repair-entry questions.

### H-E — Rotation / branch succession

**Pattern:**

- original leader weakens;
- a related branch or successor gains depth;
- causal story continues;
- total family capital remains organized.

**Supports:** rotation rather than whole-theme death.
**Counterevidence:** only one unrelated stock rises; branch strength is shallow; causal continuity ends; old core and branches all lose support.

### H-F — Fade watch / fade confirmed

**Pattern:**

- event chain weakens;
- acceptance declines across sessions;
- breadth narrows;
- leaders fail or lose support;
- repairs repeatedly fail;
- risk appetite migrates elsewhere.

**Supports:** fade-watch then, if hard conditions hold, fade-confirmed.
**Counterevidence:** new event revives causal chain; core repairs; a credible successor organizes breadth; market regime turns supportive.
**Required distinction:** one weak session, weak event continuity, or divergence is not equivalent to confirmed fade.

### H-G — Rotation chaos / attention artifact

**Pattern:**

- labels change quickly;
- no stable causal chain;
- movement follows short-lived capital rotation;
- participation is shallow;
- no credible hierarchy.

**Supports:** no-theme-lifecycle or observation-only interpretation.
**Counterevidence:** later repeated events and market payment establish a durable family.

### H-H — Data/provenance artifact

**Pattern:**

- apparent lifecycle transition relies on missing leader data, duplicate labels, stale snapshots, source gap, or version mismatch.

**Supports:** insufficient evidence, not market conclusion.
**Counterevidence:** independent source or corrected data supports the transition.
**Required result:** label missing/unavailable and preserve the question; do not convert absent evidence into a negative market fact.

## REGIME_APPLICABILITY

The same theme evidence can imply different interpretations under different regimes.

### Broad market gates

| Regime family | Lifecycle implication |
|---|---|
| Supportive risk appetite | Breadth and branch diffusion are easier to trust; weak-core repair is more plausible. |
| Neutral/choppy | Require more continuity and clearer hierarchy; expect false diffusion and rotation. |
| Downtrend rebound | Theme may remain alive but participation and position logic are constrained. |
| Adverse/bearish | Broad diffusion is less reliable; strength may be defensive, short-lived, or concentrated. |
| Crash risk | Preserve observations, but do not treat resilience alone as trade permission. |

### Structural market gates

The project's market-structure model distinguishes:

- **broad and non-shallow:** healthier broad strength;
- **broad but shallow:** fragile strength, especially when top-end depth is thin;
- **non-broad with depth:** concentrated leadership;
- **non-broad and shallow:** weak or chaotic conditions.

These structures are evidence, not final theme conclusions. A strong theme in a fragile market can still fail because the environment cannot support diffusion.

### Theme-internal gates

Before choosing among hypotheses, Julia must ask:

1. Is the causal event chain continuing, exhausted, or contradicted?
2. Is acceptance single-day or multi-day?
3. Is there breadth without depth, depth without breadth, or both?
4. Are leader, second row, branches, and laggards moving together?
5. Does repair occur before support fails?
6. Is a successor organizing capital or merely filling a label vacuum?
7. Are competing themes absorbing the same capital?
8. Are observations from the same provenance/version and exact trade date?

### Permission separation

Lifecycle state can suggest research questions such as "watch leader confirmation," "avoid laggard chase," or "test repair." It must not by itself issue a trade instruction. Trade permission requires separate gates: confirmed mainline, safe market regime, leader state, setup confirmation, and risk constraints.

## COUNTEREVIDENCE

Counterevidence must be sought actively and can defeat the favored interpretation.

1. **Event contradiction:** policy, order, technology, supply, or company fact invalidates the causal mechanism.
2. **Acceptance break:** heat or relative strength persists only while the whole market rises and disappears under neutral conditions.
3. **Breadth without depth:** many labels respond, but no credible leader or front row survives.
4. **Depth without breadth:** one leader rises while related stocks and branches do not respond.
5. **Repair failure:** weak core does not recover in its declared observation window; second attempt lacks turnover/support.
6. **Successor vacuum:** old leader fades, but no related stock organizes breadth.
7. **Branch failure:** branch rotation is shallow and the original core also loses support.
8. **Regime deterioration:** broad market or short-sentiment gate turns adverse while theme evidence was already marginal.
9. **Crowding/exhaustion:** response becomes indiscriminate laggard catch-up after prolonged recognition.
10. **Provenance failure:** transition depends on missing or stale evidence, duplicate theme identity, or mixed snapshot versions.

If counterevidence is decisive, Julia must demote or replace the favored hypothesis even if prior evidence was strong.

## FALSIFIERS

At least three concrete falsifiers are required. Each should be evaluated with a predeclared observation window and exact-date evidence.

| ID | Favored interpretation | Falsifying observation | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| F-01 | Nascent theme will form | No related-subject/stock acceptance over the declared window despite a visible stimulus | Demote to one-day event/news artifact |
| F-02 | Theme is fermenting | Multi-day acceptance score or relative strength collapses for the declared window | Return to early-trial or reject mainline candidacy |
| F-03 | Diffusion is healthy | Breadth rises while leader/front-row depth deteriorates | Reinterpret as broad-but-shallow or fragile strength |
| F-04 | Divergence is repairable | Core/leader repair fails before support break or declared timeout | Move to fade-watch or leader-failure interpretation |
| F-05 | Leader is rotating within theme | Original leader weakens and no successor/branch organizes breadth | Reinterpret as theme fade, not internal rotation |
| F-06 | Theme remains alive | Support break plus event-chain exhaustion persists across declared review window | Move to fade-confirmed/death hypothesis |
| F-07 | Lifecycle transition is real | Independent corrected source disagrees or provenance/version is missing | Return insufficient evidence; do not infer negative fact |
| F-08 | Regime permits diffusion | Broad-market breadth/depth or short-sentiment gate deteriorates | Preserve theme observation but suppress diffusion/trade inference |

## CORRECTION_PATH

1. **Record the prior interpretation and its evidence.** Do not silently replace it.
2. **Classify the new fact:** event, acceptance, hierarchy, repair, regime, source quality, or provenance.
3. **Test decisive counterevidence first.** A hard contradiction overrides a favorable score.
4. **Separate missing from negative.** Unavailable evidence leaves the hypothesis insufficient; it does not prove failure.
5. **Re-open competing hypotheses.** Demote the favored one and promote alternatives only to the degree supported.
6. **Check regime and population.** A hypothesis calibrated in supportive regimes must not be reused unchanged in adverse or crash regimes.
7. **Check identity continuity.** Theme renames, branch subjects, and leader succession must not accidentally create a new lifecycle or hide a death.
8. **Preserve the decision path.** Include why the interpretation changed, what would change it again, and which evidence remains unresolved.
9. **Keep judgment separate from action.** A corrected lifecycle state changes research questions, not automatic buy/sell behavior.
10. **Escalate genuine ambiguity.** If evidence conflicts or source truth is insufficient, mark the case for review rather than forcing a terminal state.

## RELATIONS

### GIC-002 — Leader Divergence

**Boundary:** GIC-001 interprets the whole theme family; GIC-002 interprets whether leader weakness is normal adjustment, active divergence, leader failure, or leader rotation.
**Interaction:** Leader divergence is one counterevidence/falsifier lane for theme lifecycle, but leader weakness alone cannot decide whole-theme death.
**Shared observations:** leader strength, follower response, successor formation, theme breadth, regime, and liquidity.
**Different conclusion:** GIC-001 may conclude "theme family remains alive"; GIC-002 may simultaneously conclude "old leader has failed." Both can be true if rotation is credible.

### GIC-003 — Weak-to-Strong

**Boundary:** GIC-001 asks which lifecycle state explains the theme; GIC-003 asks whether a specific weak core/stock is conditionally repairing and whether that repair is tradable.
**Interaction:** Weak-to-strong is one repair mechanism inside divergence/fade-watch. It can revive a lifecycle hypothesis, but only after confirmation and regime gates.
**Shared observations:** prior strength, weakness quality, auction/open confirmation, volume/support, leader position, theme acceptance, and market regime.
**Different conclusion:** A successful weak-to-strong setup can occur while the broader theme remains only watch-only; theme health does not automatically validate a setup.

### Market structure and regime cognition

Theme lifecycle must be read beside market breadth/depth and regime cognition. Broad-but-shallow strength, concentrated leadership, and weak/chaotic structures change the meaning of the same theme facts.

### Event and research evidence cognition

Event continuity and external research can support or contradict the causal mechanism, but they do not replace market acceptance. A convincing narrative with no market payment remains a candidate, not a confirmed lifecycle.

## EXAMPLE_REASONING_TRACES

These traces demonstrate reasoning, not mandatory conclusions.

### Trace A — New event, one strong day, unknown acceptance

**Facts:** A major policy event appears. One related stock rises sharply. Other related subjects do not respond. Market structure is broad but shallow.

**Reasoning:** The event gives causal plausibility, but hierarchy and breadth are absent. Broad-but-shallow structure lowers confidence in spontaneous diffusion. H-A and H-G remain active. Julia should request multi-day event continuity, related-subject response, turnover, and front-row formation.

**Not allowed:** "Major event + one strong stock = confirmed mainline" or "buy followers tomorrow."

### Trace B — Repeated acceptance, leader and branches emerge

**Facts:** Related events continue over several sessions. Theme relative strength persists. A credible leader appears, second-row stocks respond, and one branch begins forming. Market regime is supportive.

**Reasoning:** H-B is favored because continuity, acceptance, breadth, and hierarchy align. H-C remains possible if participation becomes indiscriminate. Julia should track whether breadth remains deeper than shallow and whether the leader survives the first divergence.

**Not allowed:** Treating the current state as guaranteed acceleration or copying a historical theme's later trajectory.

### Trace C — Core weakens in adverse regime

**Facts:** The original leader opens weak. Related stocks fall. Broad market is bearish/adverse. No successor has emerged. Event chain is stale.

**Reasoning:** H-F and H-E compete. Adverse regime makes repair less plausible, while successor vacuum argues against internal rotation. Julia should inspect support, repair attempt, branch depth, and whether event contradiction explains the weakness. GIC-002 is needed to distinguish old-leader failure from healthy rotation; GIC-003 is relevant only if repair evidence appears.

**Not allowed:** "Leader weak → theme dead" or "theme still alive → trade weak-to-strong" without separate confirmation.

## ANTI_COPY_RULES

1. Do not copy a historical theme's final outcome as the current answer.
2. Do not copy a prior trade action from a similar narrative.
3. Do not infer empirical performance from an example or replay label.
4. Do not turn lifecycle vocabulary into a deterministic state machine.
5. Do not collapse event, market acceptance, hierarchy, regime, and setup into one score.
6. Do not let missing evidence become evidence of failure.
7. Do not let a strategy hypothesis override contrary market facts.
8. Do not present a lifecycle interpretation as trade authorization.
9. Do not drop source identity, exact date, or snapshot version.
10. Do not remove competing hypotheses merely because one path is currently favored.
